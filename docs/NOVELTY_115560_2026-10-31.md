# Novelty assessment — CVE-2026-115560 → var_destroy → `zif_system`

**Date:** 2026-10-31
**Scope:** read-only public-record check. No lab, container, or application code
was changed. No publish, no external send, no vendor contact.
**Question:** is the exact composition already public in 2025–2026, and what can
this repo defensibly claim?

---

## 1. Verdict

**The core composition is already public.** The four-stage chain

```text
pre-auth upload-progress write under php_serialize
  -> victim session_start() decodes under php
  -> var_destroy re-entry UAF
  -> forged handlers with dtor_obj = zif_system
  -> RCE
```

is published in `ACD421/PHP`:

- `weapons/lab/fa_jammy.sh` — the full composition, jammy PHP 8.1.2, Apache
  mod_php, ASLR on, no gdb, `MaxRequestWorkers 4`, `uid=0(root)` on shot 1.
- `zero4_var_destroy_uaf/finish/FINISH_ALL_STATUS.md` — the four-stage
  description and receipts, dated 2026-10-24.
- `zero1_session_gaps/WRITEUP.md` — the same 115560-class injection and the
  **stock `cleanup=1` slow-upload race** on 8.5.12, dated 2026-10-21, with a
  simple destructor rather than var_destroy.

Therefore the sentence in `docs/RESEARCH_REVIEW_2026-10-30.md` §4.3 — “Nobody
has published the composition where the session decoder itself is the
var_destroy sink” — **is false and must be retracted.** So must any framing of
the composition, the forged `dtor_obj`, the handler switch, the cleanup race,
the `disable_functions` bypass, or multi-worker pinning as new.

**What is not found in the public record checked on 2026-10-31** is the
narrower end-to-end result:

- the composition on **patched 8.4.27 and 8.5.12**, not 8.1.2;
- with **default `cleanup=On`** (streaming race) combined with the var_destroy
  terminal, including the two-record seed requirement;
- on **arm64**, with **no `/proc` at fire time**, using a full-window carrier
  search;
- with the measured raw-session-input-buffer carrier and per-build constants.

That is an engineering/port result on top of a public composition, not a new
vulnerability and not a new primitive. It should be described that way.

**No new CVE.** CVE-2026-115560 is already assigned. The var_destroy UAF is
public since 2026-05-01/02 and PHP has a stated policy of not issuing CVEs for
`unserialize()` memory-corruption bugs. The `cleanup=On` race and the 115560
preconditions are public. Nothing here supports a new CVE request.

### 5.2 Abstract

> We show that the CVE-2026-115560 session-decode primitive can deliver the

### 5.3 Scope paragraph (must accompany any publication)

> Scope: PHP 8.4.27 and 8.5.12, Apache `mod_php`, arm64. The target must
> configure `session.serialize_handler` to `php_serialize` (or `php_binary`),
> switch it to `php` at runtime before `session_start()`, keep
> `session.upload_progress.enabled=On`, and load a class whose `__destruct`
> sprays a same-size string and re-enters `unserialize()`. The direct lab

> user, not a command interface. `__wakeup`/`__unserialize` are not usable
> for the re-entry.

## 6. Embargo options (no decision made here)

**Baseline reality:** the composition is public. An embargo cannot restore
compositional novelty. At most it protects the operator-specific pieces:

- the raw-session-input-buffer carrier placement and the 8.4.27 two-record
  requirement;
- the measured constants (`0x6c019`, `0x6d875`, `0x80875`; sprays 3048/8135;
  `lib−main` and `zif` offsets);
- the arm64 18-bit full-window search and its attempt/time profile;
- the exact seed/streaming timing (`8×64 KiB`, 0.6 s, `settle=0.7`).

Those are engineering details, not a vulnerability. They lower the cost of
reproduction but do not create a new bug.

### Option A — publish composition + carrier + constants now

- **What it protects:** nothing.
- **Pros:** maximum reproducibility and defensive value; honest about the
  public record; avoids the appearance of claiming novelty.
- **Cons:** publishes a working end-to-end exploit for an unpatched CVE and
  the only non-public engineering details; may increase opportunistic
  exploitation; no novelty benefit.
- **Fit:** a technical report or lab artifact where reproducibility is the
  point, with the scope paragraph in §5.3.

### Option B — publish composition + patched-build/arm64 result; withhold
carrier/constants for a fixed period

- **What it protects:** the carrier placement, constants, seed/timing, and
  arm64 search profile.
- **Pros:** establishes the narrow contribution and gives defenders the
  build/version/impact picture without handing over a turnkey exploit;
  compatible with coordinated disclosure; can be lifted when PHP ships the
  next release or after 30/60/90 days.
- **Cons:** reviewers cannot fully reproduce; “embargo” is partial and may be
  undermined by repo history, commit messages, or the public composition;
  does not restore novelty.
- **Fit:** the default recommendation if the repo wants a citable result but
  not a weapon.

### Option C — publish only a defensive/measurement note; keep the carrier
indefinitely

- **What it protects:** all non-public engineering detail.
